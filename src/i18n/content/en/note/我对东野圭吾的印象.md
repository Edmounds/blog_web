# My Impressions of Keigo Higashino
The first work by Keigo Higashino I read was probably *The Miracles of the Namiya General Store*.

I remember I was in elementary school at the time. I was spending my summer vacation at my cousin’s house, and since there were no phones to play with back then, I could only read. There happened to be a copy of *The Miracles of the Namiya General Store* there. Judging from the title alone, I thought it was going to be a light, everyday kind of book. It turned out to be quite captivating. The redemption brought about through letters sent across time left a deep impression on me, and I remembered the author.

The next time I encountered one of his books was in my second year of high school.

At the time, people in our dormitory started passing around *Journey Under the Midnight Sun*. I remember at least three people finished reading it on that Kindle. We were genuinely unable to put it down—it was almost on the same level as binge-watching *Breaking Bad*. Btw, even when I follow web novels, I’m never as eager as I was with this book. When it was my turn to read it, I would basically hurry to finish my homework during self-study periods and then secretly start reading, read while eating, and keep reading even after the dormitory lights were turned off (thanks to this backlit Kindle).

It was the first mystery novel I had read with such mature subject matter. Although it was dark, it also had a sense of realism, and the way the main plot progressed through the alternating perspectives of the male and female leads was quite interesting. More importantly, the story was genuinely full of twists and turns, and it was difficult to guess who the killer was. What felt most heartbreaking was that Ryoji Kirihara ultimately protected Yukiho by taking his own life, and Yukiho was actually able to say, expressionlessly, “I don’t know this person at all.”

The next time I encountered “Yukiho” was in *The Night of the Milky Way Train*. This story wasn’t as captivating as *Journey Under the Midnight Sun*, though it was still decent. The ending of *The Night of the Milky Way Train* was also quite similar to that of *Journey Under the Midnight Sun*: the male lead eventually realized that the female lead had been deceiving and using him all along, but in the end, he still took his own life to protect her and help her escape with a clean slate.

I won’t go into all his other books one by one. The image below shows the works by Keigo Higashino that I’ve read.

![image.png](https://img.muelsyse.us/bed/20260808043442545.png)

Keigo Higashino was a great writer. I’m grateful that he allowed someone like me, who isn’t used to reading, to focus long enough to finish an entire book. I’m also grateful for the stories he created, which stay with you long after you’ve read them.

R.I.P.
