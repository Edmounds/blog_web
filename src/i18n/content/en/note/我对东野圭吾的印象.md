# My Impression of Keigo Higashino
The first work by Keigo Higashino that I read was probably *The Miracles of the Namiya General Store*.

I remember it was when I was in elementary school. I was spending my summer vacation at my cousin’s house, and since there were no phones to play with back then, I had no choice but to read. As it happened, there was a copy of *The Miracles of the Namiya General Store* there. Judging solely by the title, I thought it would be a light, slice-of-life kind of book. It turned out to be quite fascinating. The redemption brought about through letters sent across time left a deep impression on me, and I remembered the author.

The next time I encountered his work was in my second year of high school.

At the time, people in our dormitory started passing around *Journey Under the Midnight Sun*. I remember that at least three of us read it on the same Kindle. We were genuinely so engrossed that we barely ate or slept—it was almost as intense as binge-watching *Breaking Bad*. BTW, even when I read online novels, I was never as enthusiastic as I was with this one. When it was my turn to read it, I would basically finish my homework as quickly as possible during self-study periods and then sneak a look, read while eating, and keep reading even after the dormitory lights had been turned off (thank goodness for this backlit Kindle). It was my first time reading a mystery novel with such mature subject matter. Although it was dark, it also had a certain sense of realism, and the way the main plot progressed through the alternating perspectives of the male and female leads was quite interesting. More importantly, the story was full of twists and turns, and it was very difficult to guess who the killer was. The most heartbreaking part was that Ryoji Kirihara ultimately protected Yukiho by taking his own life, only for Yukiho to say expressionlessly, “I don’t know this person at all.”

The next time I encountered “Yukiho” was in *The Phantom Night*. This story wasn’t as compelling as *Journey Under the Midnight Sun*, though it was still fairly decent. The ending of *The Phantom Night* was also very similar to that of *Journey Under the Midnight Sun*: the male protagonist eventually discovers that the female protagonist has been deceiving and using him all along, but in the end, he still takes his own life to protect her, allowing her to escape without suspicion.

I won’t go into all his other books one by one. The image below shows the works by Keigo Higashino that I’ve read.
![Works by Keigo Higashino that I’ve read|475](https://img.muelsyse.us/bed/20260808043442545.png)


Keigo Higashino was a great writer. I’m grateful to him for enabling someone like me, who isn’t accustomed to reading, to concentrate and finish an entire book. I’m also grateful for the stories he created, which stay with you long after you’ve finished them.

R.I.P.
